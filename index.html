The Films of Wes Anderson

Twelve features and one Oscar-winning short, 1996–2025

Tip: each slide has its own link. Copy the address bar to share a specific film.

About this project

This is a revised version of a TimelineJS project that originally covered ten of Wes Anderson's feature films with a one-line description and an official trailer for each. This version keeps the original structure: release dates, trailers, and a colour for each film. It also brings the filmography up to date, adds context to every slide, and fixes several accessibility and maintenance problems.

What changed

  • Complete filmography. Added Asteroid City (2023), The Phoenician Scheme (2025), and the Oscar-winning short The Wonderful Story of Henry Sugar (2023). The original stopped at 2021.
  • Richer slides. Each film now has a short synopsis, why it matters (awards, firsts, production details), and a key-facts list: distributor, writers, and principal cast.
  • Groups and eras. Films are sorted into rows by format (live action, stop-motion, short), and shaded bands mark four phases of Anderson's career. The original sheet left the Group column empty.
  • Readable colours. TimelineJS always puts white text on coloured slide backgrounds, so many of the original colours (such as baby blue #89CFF0 and pink #FFB6C1) gave text a contrast of only about 1.5:1. The new palette uses a pastel taken from each film's look, paired with dark text at 9:1 or better.
  • Stable, credited title image. Replaced a Google image-search thumbnail link (which can expire) with an openly licensed Wikimedia Commons photograph, credited under its CC BY-SA 4.0 licence.
  • Accessibility and cleanup. Added alt text and captions to all media and removed a leftover placeholder value (“asdf”) from the title row. Trailer credits now name the actual YouTube channel. All 13 trailer links were checked and work.
  • Shareable slides. Each slide has its own URL, so a link can open directly on one film.
  • Self-hosted, version-controlled data. The timeline loads from a JSON file in this repository, generated from a CSV that uses the exact TimelineJS spreadsheet columns. It no longer depends on a Google Sheet staying published, and every edit is tracked in Git.

Ideas for further expansion

  • Add slides for formative events: the 1994 Bottle Rocket short, the Hotel Chevalier prologue, and the other three Dahl shorts.
  • Use film stills or posters as slide background images. Check the licensing first, because most are copyrighted.
  • Make a companion timeline of recurring collaborators (Bill Murray, Owen Wilson, Jason Schwartzman, Roman Coppola, Alexandre Desplat, Robert Yeoman).
  • Add box-office and critics' scores, with a cited source for every figure.
  • Pair the timeline with a StoryMapJS map of filming locations: Texas, Rome, Rajasthan, Rhode Island, Görlitz, Angoulême, and Chinchón.

Data

Download the spreadsheet (CSV). It uses the official TimelineJS template columns, so you can paste it straight into a Google Sheet. You can also download the TimelineJS JSON that this page renders.

Sources & credits

Release dates are U.S. theatrical release dates (Netflix release for Henry Sugar). Film facts were compiled from the films' credits and standard references (Wikipedia, the Academy Awards database, the Berlinale and Festival de Cannes archives). Trailers are embedded from the studios' and distributors' official or licensed YouTube channels. The title photograph is by Martin Kraft (Wikimedia Commons), CC BY-SA 4.0. The timeline is built with TimelineJS by Northwestern University's Knight Lab.
